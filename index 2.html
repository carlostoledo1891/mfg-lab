Computer-assisted proof and validated numerics

I am Carlos Toledo. I work on numerical claims that have to be checkable without trusting how they were produced — my own, a published paper’s, or one a language model generated — and the answer is either a certified interval that provably contains the true value, or a plain statement that the method did not close. Each note below ships the program that produced its numbers.

Notes

  • Number theory · Erdős #290
    For even d, disc(fd) is a square exactly at d = 4k(k+1) — proved — and the five known exceptional degrees are exactly those with d ≤ 120

    A certified bracket for the constant c in van Doorn’s bound, seven times narrower than the published 0.82 < c < 0.85, and a short proof that disc(fd) is a perfect square exactly at d = 4k(k+1) — the only degrees where the Galois group can drop into the even-weight subgroup. Two of the five, d = 80 and d = 120, lie beyond the range the paper computed.

  • Independent verification
    Three AI-claimed results, re-checked in arithmetic written from scratch

    All three hold on their computational layer. The finding is what was there before: on each one, the only check on record was the author’s own.

  • Network equilibrium · reproduction
    A certified reproduction of the AIMS Mathematics 2026 multi-population Wardrop paper

    Three scenarios, three different outcomes: one solved exactly over rationals, one bounded by a Krawczyk argument, one refused because that instance admits more than one equilibrium.
