Draft prospectus 04 of 05 · Clifford / geometric algebra

Singularity-free rigid-body enclosures in rotor form

Target: Aerospace · autonomous vehicles · heavy industry simulation

Status — read this before anything below

Nothing on this page is claimed, certified, enclosed or proved. There is no kernel, no certificate, no falsifier and no ledger record behind it. It states a field, the first claim in that field that would be decidable, what a certificate would have to assert, and what would have to exist before any of it could be attempted. A prospectus that reads like a result is the defect, so this one says so at the top and is not published.

Occupancy check, 2026-07-30 — KILLED · OCCUPIED

KILLED, and the premise is self-defeating. Validated rigid-body integration is owned by CAPD, COSY INFINITY Taylor models and the Zgliczyński–Tucker school; the search returned no geometric-algebra-plus-interval work at all. But the fatal objection is structural, not bibliographic: "preserving R R† = 1 exactly" is unavailable inside an enclosure. An interval rotor is a set of multivectors, and a set cannot satisfy an equality pointwise unless it is a single point — a validated integrator's rotor set has positive width by construction. The best any method achieves is enclosing the residual, which is what a Taylor-model integrator already reports for a rotation matrix. The word doing the work in the proposal is the word that cannot survive contact with interval arithmetic.

What it is

Geometric algebra unifies scalars, vectors and higher blades in one coordinate-free system. Rotations are rotors satisfying R R† = 1 — no Euler angles, no gimbal lock, and no chart to switch between.

Why it is a frontier

Simulations that decide whether hardware is safe are trusted more than their error bounds justify. A rotor formulation makes the invariant (R R† = 1) algebraic rather than a normalisation applied after the fact.

The first decidable claim

Not a research programme — one statement that is either true or false, small enough to be attacked. Everything else follows from having one of these.

the claim

For a rigid body integrated over [0, T] from a given state: the rotor normalisation residual stays below r and the energy drift stays below r′ at every step, for all initial data in a stated box — not merely along the trajectory that was computed.

what a certificate would assert

A validated enclosure of the trajectory over the whole box, with the conservation residual evaluated pre-update — a post-update identity that is exact by construction certifies nothing.

what would falsify it

An initial condition inside the box whose integration exceeds the claimed residual, and a deliberately broken integrator that the same check must reject.

What must exist first

A rational multivector kernel, an interval rotor exponential, and a step rule whose local error is bounded rather than estimated.

The honest risk

The strongest incumbent of the five. Validated ODE integration is a developed field with serious tools — CAPD, COSY INFINITY, Taylor-model integrators, and rigorous rigid-body results behind them. The question this must survive is blunt: what does the geometric-algebra formulation certify that a Taylor-model integrator does not already certify better? If the answer is "nothing", that is the finding.

Literature occupancy

An occupancy check is a precondition, not a formality. No claim here may be called ours until a literature gate has returned a verdict on this specific niche — not on the field, which in every one of these five is old and well populated, but on the narrow question of whether certified, rigorously enclosed results already exist in it. Any of these five may be killed by that check, and killing four of five now would be a good outcome.
