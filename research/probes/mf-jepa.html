Sandbox draft 06 · Mean-field JEPA · population world models

A JEPA predictor whose output is a density, not a vector

Feasibility verdict — INFEASIBLE HERE

Mathematically interesting, and outside this repo's competence. The deliverable is a trained world model — the claim "predicts O(N) multi-agent behaviour in O(1)" is a statement about a network that has been trained and benchmarked. This tree has no GPU, no training loop, no datasets and no torch. It ships single-file zero-dependency verifiers. An idea whose proof is an experiment cannot be certified here, and dressing it as a certificate would be the exact category error the five previous prospectuses were built to avoid.

Occupancy check: Tier A ("O(N) in O(1)") is FOLKLORE — the definition of the mean-field limit, sold in arXiv:1802.05438's abstract (ICML 2018). Tier B is a DIFFERENT MECHANISM: Mei–Montanari–Nguyen and Chizat–Bach put a measure over neurons, not agents, and citing them as prior art here would repeat the crowd-aversion error. Tier C is empty because nobody needs it.

Status

Nothing here is claimed, certified or proved. No kernel, no certificate, no falsifier, no ledger record. A sandbox note. The feasibility verdict above is this repo's own judgement; the occupancy half is commissioned and provisional until it returns.

What it is

Standard JEPA predicts one latent vector from the previous one. The proposal replaces that with an HJB–FPK operator in latent space, so the predictor's output is a population density m(t,·) at a mean-field equilibrium rather than a point.

The claim, and whether it is decidable

the claim

That a latent predictor carrying an MFG equilibrium beats a vector predictor on multi-agent environments. That is an empirical claim, settled by training two models and comparing them.

is there a certificate here WE could build?

None that we could build. The certifiable fragment would be the PDE solver inside it — which is what this tree already does for MFG, and which is not the novel part of the proposal.

The honest risk

Occupancy is likely heavy: mean-field limits of neural networks are established (Mei–Montanari–Nguyen, Chizat–Bach), and MFG-based multi-agent RL exists as a field. Being wrong about that would not change the feasibility verdict.
