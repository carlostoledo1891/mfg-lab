Feasibility verdict — OCCUPIED, and infeasible here

Two independent kills. Occupancy: Wasserstein objectives in deep learning are a crowded, mature area — the WGAN lineage from 2017 and Sinkhorn divergences as differentiable losses (Genevay, Peyré, Cuturi) are standard practice, not an undiscovered frontier. Feasibility: the deliverable is again a trained model with a different loss function, which this tree cannot produce or evaluate. The "via dual Monge–Ampère" phrasing is the only part that might distinguish it from entropic practice, and that is a numerical-methods choice rather than a new object.

Occupancy check — worse than expected. LeJEPA (Balestriero & LeCun, arXiv:2511.08544) occupies the distributional-objective slot and explicitly routes around transport distances in favour of linear-cost characteristic-function matching. The proposal is the option the field's flagship considered and did not take. The dual Monge–Ampère route is separately occupied (arXiv:1908.10962). And this tree killed it twice already: CERT_FAMILY records that the 1-D scalar-index sequence module "kills Monge–Ampère" for want of a 2-D Banach algebra, and CERT_TRANSFER logs the Sinkhorn gauge-nullspace blocker as "FAR, not NEXT".

What it is

Use the W₂ distance from Monge–Kantorovich transport as the JEPA predictor loss instead of L₁/L₂/cosine, so the predictor routes latent distributions along geodesics.

The claim, and whether it is decidable

the claim

That a W₂ latent loss prevents representation collapse better than the heuristic metrics. Empirical, and settled by training.

is there a certificate here WE could build?

None. The transport machinery adjacent to this tree certifies a solved discrete instance; it says nothing about whether a loss function trains better.

The honest risk

If anything the risk runs the other way: claiming novelty here would be dismissed immediately by anyone who has read the generative-modelling literature since 2017.
