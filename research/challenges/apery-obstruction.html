FrontierMath starter report 01 of 02 · Number theory

Apéry-style irrationality proofs

FrontierMath open problem, verbatim: “Adapt Apéry's proof of the irrationality of ζ(3) to other constants.” Assessed against a proposal to use mean-field equilibrium search as the generator of the missing recurrence.

Status — read this before anything below

Nothing on this page is claimed, certified, enclosed or proved. There is no kernel, no certificate, no falsifier and no ledger record behind it. It assesses a direction and names one artifact that could be built. A prospectus that reads like a result is the defect, so this one says so at the top and is not published.

The premise correction — this is the finding

The proposal says: “The difficult part is discovering the recurrence.” For ζ(5), that is not true, and it has not been true since 2002. Zudilin published a third-order Apéry-like recursion for ζ(5) — arXiv:math/0206178, “A third-order Apéry-like recursion for ζ(5)” — with solutions whose quotients converge to ζ(5). The irrationality of ζ(5) remains open anyway. The recurrence exists; the proof does not follow from it.

A generator aimed at producing recurrences is therefore aimed at a bottleneck that is already cleared on the flagship target. Any proposal in this direction has to be re-aimed at what actually blocks, which is stated in the next section.

What actually blocks — the arithmetic, not the recurrence

An Apéry proof has three ingredients and the proposal names them correctly: a recurrence, a quantity approximating the constant extraordinarily well, and asymptotic control. What the proposal misses is that these are not three independent difficulties. The third is a race between two rates, and the race is arithmetic.

Apéry's argument produces linear forms aₙζ(3) − bₙ where aₙ and bₙ are rationals with controlled denominators — the classical dₙ³aₙ ∈ ℤ integrality. Irrationality follows only when the error decays faster than the denominators grow. Zudilin's ζ(5) recursion loses that race: the decay and growth exponents are governed by the roots of the characteristic polynomial μ³ + 2368μ² − 752μ − 16, and they do not clear the bar. The search literature records the same thing from the other side — extensive computer searching has failed to find the constants needed to push Apéry's method onto the higher odd zeta values.

why this kills the MFG generator, structurally

Irrationality turns on integrality, and no continuum limit can see integrality. The proposal's mechanism is an HJB equation whose optimal drift u*(t) yields the recurrence coefficients. An HJB drift is real-valued. It can optimise growth rates; it cannot distinguish a coefficient whose denominators clear dₙ³ from one that misses by a prime. The single property that decides an Apéry proof is invisible to the machinery proposed to find it.

This is a structural objection in the sense SCORING.json fixes: it says the thing cannot work, not that someone got there first. Under this tree's own rule, structural kills survive rescoring and occupancy kills do not. MFG-as-generator for Apéry is killed. What is not killed is on the next screen, and it is worth more.

What survives — and it is exactly this tree's product

Turn the problem around. If the bottleneck is a race between two certified exponents, then deciding whether a given recurrence wins that race is a certification problem, and certification is what this tree does. The artifact is not a prover. It is a refuter with a measured margin.

the first decidable claim

For a given Apéry-like recurrence, presented as its characteristic polynomial with integer coefficients and a denominator-growth exponent: the resulting linear forms do — or do not — satisfy the irrationality inequality, decided by certified real-root isolation of that polynomial in exact rational arithmetic.

what a certificate would assert

A certified bracket on each real root of the characteristic polynomial, obtained by Sturm sequences over exact rationals — no floating point anywhere in the decision — and hence certified upper and lower bounds on the decay and growth exponents, and the sign of their difference. The verdict is PROVED, or REFUSED together with the margin by which it missed.

the red and green controls, which already exist as mathematics

ζ(3) is the green control: Apéry's own recurrence must come out PROVED, or the artifact is wrong. ζ(5) with Zudilin's recursion is the red control: it must come out REFUSED, because irrationality of ζ(5) is open — if the artifact ever proves it, the artifact is broken. A checker that ships with a case it must fail is the falsifier discipline this tree already requires, handed to us by the literature for free.

what would falsify it

A recurrence whose exponents the checker brackets on the wrong side of the truth — detectable by mutation, since a deliberately perturbed characteristic polynomial must flip the verdict. Standard mutation-harness territory, and this tree has the harness.

What must exist first

Less than usual, which is the point of naming this one.

Scored on the two axes

N — academic novelty

ZERO. Every ingredient is classical. Apéry 1978, Zudilin 2002, Sturm 1829. Nothing here is new mathematics and the report should not pretend otherwise.

A — artifact value: does a checkable version exist?

Provisionally HIGH, and unverified. The claim would be that no zero-dependency, seconds-to-run, independently checkable decision procedure exists that takes an Apéry-like recurrence and returns a certified verdict with a margin. That claim has not been through a literature gate and must not be stated until it has. Computer algebra systems certainly compute Sturm sequences; whether anyone has shipped this specific artifact, with a refusal that reports its own margin, is exactly the narrow occupancy question the gate exists to answer.

The honest risk

The artifact may be true and boring. If the answer for every recurrence anyone cares about is already in the literature as a printed exponent, then a checker that recomputes it certifiedly adds rigour nobody was missing. The defence is the refusal margin — “ζ(5) misses by this much” is a number that points at how much a new construction would have to improve, and that is a usable research instrument rather than a re-derivation.

And the larger risk: it does not solve the FrontierMath problem. It decides candidates; it does not produce them. Nothing in this report claims a route to the irrationality of ζ(5), and the page would be dishonest if it implied one.

The general proposal, assessed separately

The supplied text's broadest idea — “MFG not as a proof technique but as a search engine for mathematics”, treating recurrences, inequalities and proof fragments as populations of interacting agents — is the most original thing in it, and it is not killed by anything above.

But it is very likely occupied, and by strong incumbents: optimisation-driven discovery of mathematical structures, verified afterwards by an exact checker, is the operating principle of FunSearch, PatternBoost and the reinforcement-learning-for-combinatorics line of work. Occupancy is not a structural kill under this tree's corrected rubric, so the direction stays open — but the differentiator cannot be “we verify the output”, because those systems already do. This needs a real literature gate before another word is written about it, and this page deliberately stops here rather than guessing the verdict.
