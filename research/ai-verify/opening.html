A certificate, and a button that breaks it

A mean-field game with congestion has a solution at one instance. You do not have to believe that. Below is the command that checks it on your machine with no dependencies, a control that makes the same certificate refuse, and the mistake this made before it was right.

What is and is not claimed here. This page re-runs a claim and its certificate, verdict PROVED, already minted in the ledger. Their identifiers are not printed here: claim and certificate ids name records that do not ship, and pointing a reader at one they cannot open is an appeal to invisible authority. It claims nothing new. Scope is one instance — A=5, γ=0.5, σ=0.5, a=½, N=20 cosine modes — and no surrounding law over any parameter is asserted.

2 · The break

The certificate closes because a radii polynomial is negative on a bracket of radii and the map is a strict contraction there. Those are two conditions, not one. Press break it: the radius walks up the bracket until p(r) is still negative but κ crosses 1 — the old acceptance test passes and the theorem does not apply. Nothing degrades or warns; it refuses.

Certified bounds, copied from the certificate record and gated against it, digit for digit, by this page's own battery: Y₀ = 3.0067633837811714e-14, Z₁ = 0.9271316033691069, Z₂ = 63.43867878981078, giving r = 4.332607412139519e-13 and density min m ≥ 0.5889178299249256 over the whole ball.
That gate is 21 checks, and it has been red. When it was wired into the ritual on 7 August 2026 it reported 19 of 21 — not because the certificate had drifted but because this page carried two hashes the certificate does not pin. Re-verifying the certificate against the bytes on disk settled which side was stale: the certificate was current, the page was wrong. The page was corrected, and the gate has reported 21 checks, 0 failures since.

3 · What this got wrong

Until 30 July 2026 the acceptance test was p(r) < 0, and the code said it had shown a contraction. It had not. p(r) < 0 gives a self-map of the ball, and a self-map of a ball in an infinite-dimensional Banach space need not have a fixed point at all — the two conditions part company at the vertex of p, where the contraction factor κ = Z₁ + Z₂r equals exactly 1. A concrete witness sits in the source today: a triple whose p(r) < 0 holds while κ = 1.0166, which the old test would have accepted and which proves nothing. No gate caught this and none could have — every test passed, because the code did exactly what it was written to do; it was found by reading the acceptance condition against the theorem it was supposed to be enforcing. The fix checks κ < 1 at the smallest closing radius. At the instance above κ = 0.92713…, so this enclosure survived the correction. It did not have to, and that is the point.
