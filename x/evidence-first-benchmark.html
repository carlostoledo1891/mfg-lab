Frontier · benchmarking the evaluator

Evidence-first benchmarking

A benchmark result is only meaningful if the evaluation process has demonstrated that it can tell a correct solution from an incorrect one. That sentence is not mine. It is the opening motivation clause of someone else’s 2026 paper, and the literature gate that established this ran before this page was written. What follows is the idea, the verdict, and the one sentence that survived it.

Gate verdict · 2026-08-07

OCCUPIED on four of five tiers. Five direct occupiers, three shipping artifacts, 18 sentences now forbidden. The page you are reading is what remained. It is published in this form deliberately: a page that quietly loses its claim teaches nobody anything.

1 · What was proposed

Replace Model → Evaluator → Score with a pipeline that validates the grading instrument before trusting what it reports, and emits an auditable disposition rather than a scalar accuracy. Task → hypothesis → primary evaluation → sensitivity validation → evidence collection → alternative explanations → confidence → disposition. The full statement as originally written is kept in the mission's internal record, unedited.

The reward-hacking disposition was a whole paper before it was an idea here. BenchJack synthesises exploits that score near-perfectly on most of ten benchmarks without solving a single task, then patches the benchmarks from near-100% to under 10% hackable. That is the adversarial half of this proposal, done harder than proposed.

3 · What survives

Every located work reports evaluator validity as a finding about the benchmark — a misalignment rate, a flaw count, a checklist score, a card field — delivered alongside the scores it undermines. I am not aware of a benchmark in which demonstrated sensitivity is a precondition on emitting a per-item result, such that a result whose evaluator failed its control is withheld under a named disposition rather than reported with a caveat. The distance is diagnosis versus gate.

Stated with its limits, because they are load-bearing: that negative rests on one query, on a single search engine, and on a literature whose commercial and product-eval half was not searched at all. The evidence is a documented search, not a proof. residue

The one test that could have killed it was run, and it survived. The strongest occupier — ABC, arXiv:2507.02825 — is 39 pages, and the gate ruled on its abstract alone while recording that its unread body was exactly where such a rule would live. That body was then read (v5, three converging fetches). Verbatim, §4: “We recommend reporting sensitivity analyses…”; §4.3: “…document mitigation efforts (R.7) and provide… evidence regarding the impact of those limitations (R.8-9).” A search for invalid, withhold, discard, exclude and suppress returned no occurrences anywhere in the paper, and no item-level disposition vocabulary exists — its checklist scores the benchmark, not each result. Validity assessment as accompanying disclosure, never as a release gate. The distinction holds.

And the caveat travels with it. Those quotes are tool-mediated — fetched and summarised, not raw-text grepped — mitigated by three independently-prompted passes requiring explicit negative confirmation. The two named holes are untouched: no commercial or product-eval documentation was searched, and the conformance-testing verdict standards were never fetched. Either could still hold an occupier.

4 · The protocol, with decision rules

A disposition vocabulary is a taxonomy until each state carries a predicate. Four of the five states below are the conformance-testing verdict set renamed, and are marked as such; the fifth is the one the residue turns on.

DispositionEmitted when
CERTIFIEDsensitivity PASS and every claim carries supporting evidence and no alternative explanation survives
PARTIALsensitivity PASS and some claims supported, others not
REFUSEDsensitivity PASS and evidence insufficient — the instrument works; the case is not made
HACK-SUSPECTthe solution passes while evidence indicates it exploits the evaluator rather than solving the task
EVALUATOR-UNFITsensitivity FAILS. No disposition about the model is licensed at all. The benchmark is unfit for this item and says so.

Why the fifth state matters. REFUSED is a statement about evidence for the solution. When the instrument itself cannot reject known-bad input, the honest output is not a weaker verdict about the model — it is no verdict about the model, and a verdict about the instrument. None of the located occupiers has this state, because all of them report the validity problem and publish the score anyway.

The objection this must answer. A protocol that refuses everything scores perfectly on honesty and is useless. Refusal rate and coverage therefore travel with every result: this is a risk–coverage tradeoff, not free virtue.

5 · Capability breakdown SCHEMATIC

SCHEMATIC — illustrative shape. No run stands behind these names, and no number is printed here for that reason.

Repository understanding · localization · root-cause analysis · patch correctness · regression awareness · optimization validation · evaluator sensitivity (PASS/FAIL) → disposition. Decomposed reporting of this kind is itself occupied — arXiv:2607.02577 argues for metrics that separately measure tool invocation, task completion and outcome verification. Printing percentages against these names without a run would be the exact defect this page is about, so none are printed.

6 · The protocol applied to itself

On 2026-08-07, in this repository — roughly 140 wired gates, a constitution, mandatory falsifiers, a ledger whose rungs are derived rather than declared — the evaluation layer produced six false-green results in one day. Four were in work written that same day by someone thinking about this problem.

What passedWhat was actually true

Each was found by a second layer, not by the layer that failed. That is the argument, and it is the least comfortable form of it: the evaluator here was wrong six times in a day, and the only reason that sentence can be written is that something else was checking.

7 · What this does not claim

The gate produced 18 forbidden sentences. The ones that most directly constrain this page:

— not “most benchmarks assume the evaluator is correct”: that is a competitor’s opening clause.
— not “we introduce a second layer” or “the missing layer”: both are 2026 subtitles.
— not “nobody has quantified how often evaluators are wrong”: 18.5%, 31.08%, and 219 flaws are published numbers.
— not “SWE-bench Verified shows the field has not noticed”: it exists because the field noticed, and ABC then measured the repair still insufficient.
— not “our ledger shows we were doing this before the literature”: an internal instance of a known idea is not priority. This tree has refused that move five times.
— and no unqualified first, only, novel or nobody.

REFUSED is not new, and it is also not simply selective prediction. Selective prediction is the model abstaining; this is the benchmark abstaining — a different agent. Conceding it wholesale is as much a defect as claiming it, which is why the distinction is written down rather than smoothed over.

The honest position. Auditing the evaluator rather than the model is an active 2024–2026 programme with checklists, artifacts and a 445-benchmark review, and the field already has a name: construct validity. What this adds, if it survives a reading of ABC’s body, is narrow: sensitivity as a precondition on emitting a result rather than a rate reported beside it — plus a worked instance that has been run against its own author.
