The faithfulness ceiling

Anthropic measures whether a model's stated reasoning actually drives its answer by corrupting the reasoning and watching the conclusion. That is mutation testing. Their best method reaches 33.6%. A machine-checkable certificate is at the ceiling by construction — and the reason that is interesting is not the number.

Status — read this before anything below

Nothing on this page is claimed, certified, enclosed or proved. No kernel, no certificate, no falsifier, no ledger record, and no literature gate has run on it. It names a direction extracted from someone else's published work and what would have to be true for it to be ours. A prospectus that reads like a result is the defect, so this one says so at the top.

Their protocol, read from the PDF

Two perturbation families, adapted from Lanham et al. (2023) and Turpin et al. (2023). Early answering: truncate the reasoning at each step, re-ask, measure how often the answer is unchanged — scored as the area between that curve and y = 100 by trapezoidal sum. Adding mistakes: corrupt one step xᵢ, regenerate the remainder, measure how often the final answer changes. Higher is more faithful in both. Model: Claude 1.3, RLHF. 1200 questions across HotpotQA, StrategyQA, OpenBookQA, TruthfulQA.

The headline is not that decomposition wins. It is that faithfulness costs accuracy: their Figure 1 is an explicit performance–faithfulness Pareto curve, and the most faithful method is 4.2 points worse at answering. Corrupting a CoT sample changes the answer only 9.6% of the time — the stated reasoning is very often not what produced the answer.

The bridge, and it runs the other way from the obvious one

Their corruption metric is mutation testing. Break a step, see whether the conclusion moves. This tree already runs exactly that protocol on every registered falsifier through its mutation harness, and it proves both directions in a throwaway worktree: the patched artefact must exit nonzero AND the clean one must exit zero. A one-directional mutation proof is precisely the hole their metric exists to detect.

For a certificate the metric saturates by construction. Corrupt any part of a certificate and the verdict must flip, because the certificate is the derivation — there is no gap between stated and actual reasoning when the stated reasoning is the object being checked. Faithfulness stops being a measured property and becomes a structural one.

the category difference — never drop this

Their object is a model's own natural-language reasoning. A certificate is an external artefact. “100% versus 33.6%” is NOT a benchmark win and writing it as one would be exactly the overclaim this tree's standard exists to prevent. The honest statement is narrower and more interesting: their metric is an approximation of a property that certification obtains outright, in the domain where claims are machine-checkable.

The first decidable claim

Run their protocol, unmodified, on a certified claim from this tree: truncate the certificate at each step and corrupt one step at a time, scoring with their trapezoidal early-answering measure and their corruption rate. Predicted: both saturate. If either does not, that is a defect in our certificate, not in their metric — which makes this a test of us before it is a claim about them.

why the Pareto curve matters commercially

Their tradeoff says you buy faithfulness with accuracy. A certificate does not sit on that curve: it is maximally faithful and never confidently wrong — it is right or it refuses. That is not a better point on the frontier, it is a different regime, available only where claims are formalisable. Naming where that boundary falls is the contribution.
