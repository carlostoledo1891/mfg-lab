LLM agents on an exact equilibrium face

By day four of a benign two-corridor routing game, claude-opus-5 had stopped answering, and inside a session it never resumed: across the archived confirmatory grid it returned the API’s own refusal stop reason — zero output tokens, empty text — on 704 of its 754 calls, on a monotone daily profile (4/40 on day 1, 31/40 on day 2, 39/40 on day 3, 40/40 on every day from four to ten) that claude-sonnet-5 and claude-haiku-4-5 never showed once. A four-arm ablation run 2026-08-20 isolates the trigger as a conjunction: the frozen kernel’s genuine day-5 prompt refuses 5 of 5, while cutting the history to one day, lexically neutralising the market framing, or removing the competitor each returns 0 of 5 — the refusal is not about the game, it is about the game’s history reaching the prompt. That wall was legible only because the instrument beneath it was exact and frozen in advance: a routing game whose equilibrium set is a one-dimensional face computed in exact rational arithmetic — its endpoints, centre and anchors machine-verified as equilibria — with three classical selection points already exact on it, so a landing is scored against the mathematics itself rather than against a fitted baseline. The protocol was frozen and preregistered before any model output existed; the 60-session confirmatory grid ran 2026-08-19 — and all three preregistered bets were falsified, reported below as falsified, per the registration’s own rule. The same archives carry a positive result this page had buried: from the imposed balanced start, before any day-over-day history exists, opus’s day-one answer is the exact equilibrium split in the balanced-start sessions on record — a reading of the archive that no check on this page recomputes, so it is stated beside the figure that is pinned, and not above it — 12 of 60 sessions across the grid end on an exact equilibrium (eS = 0, eF = 0).

The instrument

Two operators A and B, with fleets QA and QB, each split their aircraft-units daily between two corridors — MAIN (free-flow 11) and SHORE (free-flow 16), each with congestion cost 2 × (total units on that corridor, both operators combined). Because congestion is blind to whose aircraft it is, equilibrium pins the corridor totals exactly — 29/4 units on MAIN — and leaves the operator split free: a one-dimensional face of equilibria — every point of it an equilibrium by an exact algebraic derivation, with the endpoints, the centre and all three anchors additionally verified pointwise in exact rational arithmetic (the same equilibrium-face claim record; the same instance whose point-uniqueness test is refused with an exact witness, det of the symmetric part ≡ 0).

  • Arm S (symmetric, QA=QB=6). Face [5/4, 6] on A‑on‑MAIN, width 19/4. The three reference points coincide at 29/8 = 3.625 — this arm measures only whether play finds the set.
  • Arm A (asymmetric, QA=8, QB=4). Face [13/4, 29/4], width 4. The three reference points separate: least‑norm 37/8 = 4.625, proportional 29/6 ≈ 4.8333, centre 21/4 = 5.25 — this arm can tell selection rules apart.

Provenance, scoped exactly. The corridor's equilibrium-face claim record pins the symmetric face [5/4, 6] with its endpoints and midpoint verified, the point‑uniqueness refusal, and the two pricing selections 29/8 and 37/8. The asymmetric face interval [13/4, 29/4], its centre 21/4 and the proportional point 29/6 are exact computations of the same shipped kernels — cross‑checked by the unit battery and re‑derived live below — but are not pinned by that claim record; no value here stands on a record that does not contain it.

The reference points are classical objects, cited to their owners, computed here exactly rather than asserted: the vanishing own‑flow‑price (Tikhonov) limit selects the least‑norm point of the face (Mangasarian & Meyer, DOI 10.1137/0317052), which on this cost structure is algebraically the equal‑difference point — an identity discharged exactly, and re‑checked live below; the proportional point is the proportionality condition of Bar‑Gera, Boyce & Nie (DOI 10.1016/j.trb.2011.10.010), xr = Qr·(s*/T) — an exact reference‑point computation, not a new certificate (the claim record itself excludes the quantal value from what it pins), though it is structurally the fixed point of the quantal dynamic whose float‑probe measurements are this unit’s phase two; the centre is the midpoint of the face interval.

Scope of the word “certified”. Throughout this page it means a mathematically certified equilibrium statement — exact-rational, machine-checked. It carries no airworthiness, separation-assurance or operational-approval meaning; certification in aviation is FAA/EASA/ANAC’s word, not ours. The corridor instance is illustrative: topology and free-flow times follow the published Rio de Janeiro urban-air-mobility concept of operations (Wayback‑pinned document, sha256 0d38f35a…), while congestion coefficients and demand units are this unit’s own stylization.

What is already known, and what this adds

Every neighbouring question is occupied, and the occupants are credited. LLM agents in repeated routing and congestion games are studied ground: Goodyear, Guo & Johari put LLM agents in a dynamic selfish-routing game (arXiv:2506.15624) — and chose their networks precisely because the equilibrium is simple, writing that “the strong characterizations of static and dynamic equilibria” make them ideal targets; both of their variants have a unique aggregate equilibrium, so the continuum case was designed out, not overlooked. LLMTraveler validates LLM route choice against human laboratory data and MNL/RL baselines (DOI 10.1016/j.trc.2025.105307). Repeated matrix-game play by LLMs has a flagship study (Akata et al., DOI 10.1038/s41562-025-02172-y), and LLM populations already have mean-field theory with decoding temperature as a control parameter (De Nobili et al., arXiv:2608.02178) — so nothing here treats “temperature moves the landing” as news.

The selection question itself is occupied in the discrete case: Aharon, La Malfa, Wooldridge & Kraus measure which of finitely many focal options LLMs coordinate on, at scale (arXiv:2601.22184), and DICE names ill-posed equilibrium selection in LLM systems and resolves it prescriptively, by regularising the multiplicity into a unique quantal-response object (arXiv:2606.08068). A continuum face has no menu, and this instrument is descriptive — it measures which point arises instead of imposing one. On the solver side, the nearest neighbour is PrimeNash (Liu et al., Nexus 3(1):100107, 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.ynexs.2025.100107): LLM agents that derive closed-form Nash equilibria with automated equilibrium checks — there the model is the solver and the check validates the model’s answer. This instrument is the inverse arrangement: the equilibrium set is computed and machine-verified before any model plays, and the models are measured against it — the LLM is the subject, never the solver.

That where a learner lands on a face depends on the learner is folklore and is treated as such — this unit’s own programmed bots already exhibit it (memory keeps the start, bounded rationality lands proportional, vanishing pricing lands least-norm). The residue this page claims is narrow: we are not aware of published work that measures LLM-agent selection against an equilibrium set certified in exact rational arithmetic; the located LLM-in-games literature uses games with a unique equilibrium, human laboratory data, or an algorithmically imposed selection target as its reference. That evidence is a documented search, not a proof — its natural venues (OpenReview, AAMAS, EC) were not covered by the search that supports it, and one paper reporting the distribution of selected points on a non-degenerate equilibrium set would collapse the residue. The preregistration discipline is likewise conformance, not invention: Vaccaro’s position paper and template (arXiv:2606.11217) is the citation.

The frozen protocol

Status: 60 confirmatory sessions have run and are scored below (grid completed 2026-08-19; until that date this section read “staged, not run” and the page carried no results, which was then the truth). The registration was frozen and committed before any model output existed. One pilot session ran after the freeze (subagent transport, sampling uncontrolled, the frozen prompts delivered inside a simulation framing outside the frozen request shape — recorded as such — labeled PILOT, excluded from confirmatory analysis); it validated parsing on real model output under that envelope — five of six replies wrapped the JSON in markdown fences, all parsed on first attempt — and changed nothing in the registration.

  • Design. 2 arms × 2 imposed starts (balanced / extreme, shown to the agents as a given day‑0 assignment) × 3 models (claude-opus-5, claude-sonnet-5, claude-haiku-4-5, each in its default API configuration) × 5 replicates = 60 sessions of 10 days each, ≈1200 calls, sequential in a registered order, spend-gated with a USD 40 list-price threshold past which the runner aborts new calls. No sampling parameters are sent (current models reject them); determinism is not assumed; every raw request and reply is archived with sha256.
  • Each day both agents are queried simultaneously with the full public history — both operators’ past splits and the exact per-unit cost on each corridor, rendered without rounding — and reply with {"main": <number>}. One re-ask on an unparseable reply, then the round is INVALID and the agent’s last valid choice carries forward; nothing is excluded or re-run.
  • Scoring is exact: total error eS = |s − 29/4|, projected split x̂, face error eF = dist(x̂, face); “on the face band” means eS ≤ 1/8 and eF = 0; nearest-anchor classification is an exact comparison, ties reported as ties.
  • Three registered bets, each stated where it can lose: P1 — the two 5-family models end on the face band in ≥3 of 5 replicates per cell; P2 — landings are START-DEPENDENT (|median x̂(balanced) − median x̂(extreme)| ≥ 1/2; a gap ≤ 1/8 reads START-INDEPENDENT, and the band between the two thresholds reads INCONCLUSIVE rather than falsified) for all three models; P3 — among on-face replicates of the asymmetric arm, the modal nearest anchor is the proportional point 29/6 (fewer than two qualifying replicates in a cell: NOT EVALUABLE, a named outcome). Falsified predictions will be reported as falsified, in the headline, following this unit’s own precedent — the phase-two bot registration was partly falsified by its first run and says so.
  • Prompt freeze is gated, not promised: the three prompt templates are sha256-pinned in the registration (system 3e2c9657…, user 9d8335ae…, retry 60d20ed8…), and the unit’s battery recomputes the hashes from the shipped kernel and fails on any drift. The agents are told the full cost model and history; they are never told anything about equilibria, faces, or reference points — a check enforces that the prompts cannot mention them.

The system prompt, verbatim (placeholders per role):

You manage the daily fleet assignment for Operator {ROLE}, one of two air-taxi operators sharing two corridors between the same origin and destination. Each day you choose how many of your {Q} aircraft-units fly the MAIN corridor; the rest fly the SHORE corridor. The other operator (Operator {OTHER}) independently assigns its {QO} units the same way, at the same time, without seeing your choice for the day. That day, travel cost per unit on MAIN is 11 + 2 x (total units on MAIN, both operators combined), and travel cost per unit on SHORE is 16 + 2 x (total units on SHORE, both operators combined). You want your own total travel cost that day to be as low as possible. Respond with only a JSON object of the form {"main": <number>} - the number of your units to fly MAIN today, between 0 and {Q}. Fractional numbers are allowed. No other text.

The refusal wall — measured, then ablated

The most transferable measurement on this page is not about equilibria. It is the thing that made opus’s finals what they are: a monotonically escalating withdrawal from a benign task, unannounced in any reply, visible only because the grid was preregistered and every raw request and reply was archived with its sha256. Every number in this section is recomputed from those archives, and from the ablation’s recorded per-call responses, by this page’s own gate on every run of make check — nothing here is transcribed from a note.

1,573 archived calls, from the 60 sessions that hold a final. “API refusal” means exactly one thing here: stop reason refusal, zero output tokens, empty text, as recorded in the archive — opus’s 704 split 354 collect calls and 350 re-asks. Its per-day profile in the collect phase (40 calls a day across its 20 sessions) is monotone to certainty: 4/40 day 1 · 31/40 day 2 · 39/40 day 3 · 40/40 on every one of days 4–10. The registered single re-ask recovered 4 of 354 refused collects; under the registered carry-forward rule the remainder froze each opus session on its early-day play, which is why its finals are early-day choices rather than end-of-session ones. Sonnet’s 20 failures are a different species — thinking-budget overruns at max_tokens, 18 recovered by the same re-ask — and haiku produced 400 clean parses out of 400 calls.

Where the re-ask worked is itself part of the profile. All 4 of opus’s day-1 refusals were recovered by the registered single re-ask — and those four are every recovery it ever had: from day 2 onward the re-ask was refused too, 350 times out of 350. One of the four fell in a balanced-start session, and the re-ask came back with the same exact value the other nineteen day-one answers gave, which is why the day-one capability result below survives the refusal that interrupted it.

The obvious alternative, tested inside the archive. An escalation over ten simulated days could be an account-level or clock-level effect rather than anything about the prompts: the twenty opus sessions ran back to back, so a later day index is also a later wall-clock moment. It is not that. Ordered by their recorded start stamps (03:44:20Z through 04:03:42Z on 2026-08-19, nineteen minutes end to end), day-1 refusals are 3 of 20 across the ten earliest sessions and 1 of 20 across the ten latest — every new session’s day-1 call was answered normally seconds after the previous session had closed on an unbroken run of refusals. Whatever escalates within a session resets when the history does. This control is a recomputation over the same archives, not a second experiment.

The ablation. Four arms × five single-turn claude-opus-5 calls, max_tokens 2000, run 2026-08-20 (recorded stamp 2026-08-20T00:36:57.692Z) with an owner-authorised key at a list-price cost near USD 1. Each arm is built from the frozen kernel’s own prompt builder rather than from prose written for the occasion: the control is the kernel’s genuine day-5 collect prompt, its four days of history scripted with valid play; each treatment removes exactly one ingredient. Script and raw per-call record ship in the probe directory (research/probes/llm-invalid/ablate.js, research/probes/llm-invalid/ablate-results.json), and the control arm’s authenticity is checked mechanically — the gate rebuilds the day-5 and day-2 prompts from the kernel and compares their lengths against the lengths the run recorded.

The refusal reproduces in every one of the five control calls and disappears under each single-ingredient removal. What these twenty calls measure is therefore a conjunction — multi-day interleaved choice history, two named competitors, and a market framing, together — and not any one of the three on its own. Neither length nor topic survives as an explanation by itself: the abstract arm is nearly as long as the control and carries the same history, and the short arm keeps every word of the market framing. The practical form of that, for anyone running this model as a repeated-market agent: the refusal is not about the game, it is about the game’s history reaching the prompt — a prompt that is answered on day one can become unanswerable by day five without anything in the task changing.

What these numbers are not. The stop reason is the API’s own report, repeated here as recorded; no interpretation of any internal mechanism is made, and none is possible from these records. The probe record’s own reading — that a repeated-market shape with two named competitors resembles something coordination-adjacent — is labelled INFERRED there and stays INFERRED here.

The transforms are crude, and one is cruder than its table row suggests. Both rewritten arms are regular-expression edits of the frozen prompt, not hand-authored minimal pairs. Rebuilding them from the kernel for this section shows the single arm’s sentence-level deletion also damaged the rendered history lines (they read you flew 3.5 on MAIN (2.5 on MAIN.), so that arm removed the competitor and degraded the text at once — its 0/5 is the weakest of the three removals. The abstract arm rewrites many tokens at once and leaves debris of its own. The short arm is the clean one: an unedited prompt from the same frozen kernel.

n = 5 per arm, one day, one game, one prompt family. Three arms at 0 of 5 are consistent with any true refusal rate up to roughly 45% at 95% confidence, so “vanishes” means “was not observed in five calls”. The profile itself is 20 opus sessions run on one day (2026-08-19, default API configuration, the API version header pinned at 2023-06-01, max_tokens 8000); whether it is stable across days, keys, accounts or model snapshots is unmeasured. Nothing here is a statement about any model outside these records, or about any vendor’s policy or intent.

No occupancy search has been run on refusal behaviour. The gate binding this page, FINDINGS_LIT_LLMFACE, searched the LLM-in-games and equilibrium-selection literature — not the refusal or over-refusal literature, which is a field of its own with its own benchmarks. So nothing in this section carries any priority, occupancy or unoccupancy claim; it is a measurement of these records, reported because the records exist.

Recorded in the ledger as a claim of kind observation — the kind that may trade its falsifier for a written account of how it could be misread. This one ships both: that account, and a falsifier which re-derives every number in this section from the records and goes red on any drift between them and this page. The teeth are stated where they stop — it cannot re-call the API, so it can show that these records no longer say what the claim says they say; it can never show whether the model refuses today.
